# CyberSafe SW brand guide

## Direction

Elegant, practical and welcoming. The visual language connects everyday accounts, devices and files, rather than suggesting hackers or complex enterprise security. The main product is always the **Cyber Security Check-Up**.

## Identity

The custom geometric mark uses three linked angular forms and negative space suggesting a flowing SW path. It is original SVG artwork, rather than a library shield. `public/assets/logo.svg` is the horizontal identity; `favicon.svg` is the compact dark-tile version. The shared website wordmark uses the same mark with live type. Keep enough clear space around it to fit half the mark's height. Do not stretch, rotate the full logo, add effects or substitute a padlock.

Use navy type and deep-green mark on light surfaces, and white type with pale teal mark on navy. The mark can stand alone for a social avatar if the surrounding context identifies the business. Keep the wordmark readable at small sizes. Printed proofs should be checked before ordering marketing materials.

## Typography

**Arial/Helvetica/system sans** for navigation, copy and clear UI. **Georgia italic** for editorial accents in major headings. System fonts are deliberate: no remote font requests, licensing downloads or font-loading shifts. Main body copy is generally 16–20 px; readable service descriptions are 14–16 px. Small labels identify supporting details, not critical instructions. The miniature report is an illustration, with its full readable version one click away.

Use one italic accent within a major heading rather than italicising paragraphs. Headings use restrained negative tracking and approximately 1.1–1.25 line height; body text uses 1.65–1.85. Mobile headings are recomposed, not simply scaled down.

## Layout, buttons and icons

Desktop content is capped at 1240 px. Main side spacing is 52 px desktop, 32 px tablet and 20 px mobile. Section spacing scales around 64–108 px. Use a mix of editorial sections, ruled questions, the report feature, service cards and quiet timelines; don't repeat the same grid for every subject.

Cards use 12 px radii, 1 px borders and 26–30 px padding. Buttons use 6 px radii and comfortable 48–54 px heights. Primary buttons are navy on light backgrounds and teal on dark backgrounds. Secondary actions use quiet text links or an outlined button. Hover lift is at most 4 px; focus rings are always visible. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

Icons share a 24 px viewBox and 1.6 px rounded stroke. They are original, simple inline SVG drawings. Do not combine them with unrelated filled or neon icon sets.

## Graphics and reports

Fine concentric rings, connected lines and restrained angular geometry make the brand recognisable. Use lines at low contrast behind foreground content. Avoid glowing locks, Matrix code, hooded figures and decorative spinning icons.

The sample report uses navy, paper and the three status colours. Preserve the large editorial title, labelled priorities, ample margins and fictional-example notice. Every PDF page must carry SAMPLE REPORT - FOR ILLUSTRATION ONLY. Never turn the fictional environment into a testimonial or case study.

## Social and future materials

The reusable `social-preview.svg` is 1200 × 630, with the identity top left, a plain-language headline, geometric motif to the right and a local footer. The included PNG is the actual OG/Twitter image. Each article has a matching SVG/PNG variant. Keep headlines short, avoid status badges suggesting certification, and use genuine service information.

For an Instagram square, adapt to 1080 × 1080 with at least 80 px safe margins and one useful point per panel. Carry the same navy/teal palette and typography hierarchy into business cards, email signatures and report covers. Confirm real contact and social details before including them. A real owner photograph may replace the J monogram panel later; do not invent a portrait or credentials.
